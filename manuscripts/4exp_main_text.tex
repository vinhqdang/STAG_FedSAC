\paragraph{Learned DRL Baselines and Stability Analysis} 
STAG-FedSAC outperforms the learned DRL baselines: it improves reward by $+56.9\%$ over FedDDPG ($\mFedDDPGReward$) and by $+68.5\%$ over LSTM-DRL ($\mLSTMDRLReward$), while cutting latency by $40.5\%$ (from $184.8$~ms) and $44.7\%$ (from $198.9$~ms), respectively. Table~\ref{tab:stats} reports the paired difference statistics across matched random seeds; all paired differences against the learned baselines are strictly positive with bootstrap intervals entirely bounded away from zero.

A main factor in the poor performance of FedDDPG and LSTM-DRL appears to be \emph{policy instability and frequent channel switching}. As shown in Table~\ref{tab:main}, FedDDPG and LSTM-DRL change the operating channel of a fraction $\mFedDDPGSwitch$ and $\mLSTMDRLSwitch$ of the APs per control interval, respectively. In a realistic deployment with MAC contention and channel-switch outages (Section~\ref{sec:deploy}), frequent channel changes incur severe service interruption ($t_{\mathrm{sw}}=3$~s) and queuing delays that reduce user throughput and increase latency. A plausible explanation, which we did not test directly, is that the DDPG baselines lack entropy regularisation and proximal constraints, so that policy updates drift away from the coordinated incumbent frequency plan. 

In contrast, STAG-FedSAC changes far fewer channels ($\mPropSwitch$ switches/AP/step, about twice the $\mSSFSwitch$ of the static plan, which only pays the initial reconfiguration). We attribute this to properties of SAC (entropy regularisation, personal heads) and to the initialisation at the incumbent configuration, but we did not run an experiment that isolates these factors, and we did not analyse which power or airtime adjustments the learned policy makes.

Figure~\ref{fig:conv} illustrates the convergence trajectories across training episodes. Both DDPG baselines rapidly degrade immediately after the exploration warm-up phase ($200$ episodes), falling into high-latency sub-optimal policies. In contrast, the validation reward of STAG-FedSAC stays close to the level of the incumbent plan throughout training, without a clear upward trend (the latent-embedding configuration with $\eta=0.1$ declines, Section~\ref{sec:exp-abl}). A perfect-information greedy optimiser (not deployable; no switching cost) evaluated on test episodes reaches a reward of $\oracleReward$ ($+\oracleGainPct\%$ above SSF), so headroom exists, but the learned controllers realise little of it.
