To evaluate system resilience under varying physical and medium conditions, Table~\ref{tab:robust} reports performance across different MAC contention regimes and channel-switching outage durations.

\paragraph{Impact of MAC-Layer Contention} 
Evaluating policies in an idealized contention-free setting yields artificially low latency: SSF latency drops from $\mSSFLat$~ms to $\mNoMacEvalSSFLat$~ms and STAG-FedSAC latency drops from $\mFullLat$~ms to $\mNoMacEvalFullLat$~ms (a factor of approximately $1.8\times$), while reward increases from $\mFullReward$ to $\mNoMacEvalFullReward$. This quantitative gap demonstrates that neglecting medium contention and queuing dynamics understates network latency by approximately $45\%$, emphasizing the critical necessity of realistic MAC-layer modeling for wireless resource evaluation.

Crucially, policies \emph{trained} without MAC contention awareness and subsequently evaluated in the realistic contention environment suffer severe performance degradation: STAG-FedSAC drops from $\mFullReward$ to $\mNoMacFullReward$ with latency spiking to $217$~ms, while FedDDPG drops from $\mFedDDPGReward$ to $\mNoMacFedDDPGReward$ with latency escalating to $257$~ms. This proves that contention-unaware policies converge to over-aggressive transmission plans that exacerbate packet collisions in high-density carrier-sense domains, demonstrating that DRL agents must be trained directly with contention awareness to ensure real-world viability.

\paragraph{Sensitivity to Channel-Switching Outages} 
Evaluating STAG-FedSAC across channel-switch outage durations $t_{\mathrm{sw}} \in \{0, 3, 10\}$~s yields rewards of $\mNoSwEvalFullReward$, $\mFullReward$, and $\mSwTenEvalFullReward$, with corresponding latencies of $\mNoSwEvalFullLat$, $\mFullLat$, and $\mSwTenEvalFullLat$~ms. The switching penalty disproportionately impacts unstable policies: FedDDPG, which reassigns channels on over $75\%$ of APs per interval, degrades dramatically from $\mNoSwEvalFedDDPGReward$ down to $\mSwTenEvalFedDDPGReward$ as $t_{\mathrm{sw}}$ rises from $0$ to $10$~s, with latency escalating to $269.6$~ms. In contrast, because STAG-FedSAC maintains disciplined, stable channel allocations ($\mPropSwitch$ switches/AP/step), it preserves robust quality of service across all outage regimes.
