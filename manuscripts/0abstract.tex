\begin{abstract}
High-density transit venues---such as airport terminals, railway stations, and bus interchanges---experience severe, schedule-driven congestion where passenger crowds gather predictably around arrival and departure events. Commercial WLAN controllers are largely reactive: they rebalance radio resources after congestion has already degraded service. In this work, we propose \textbf{STAG-FedSAC}, an end-to-end framework for proactive, schedule-driven WiFi radio resource management. STAG-FedSAC integrates three coordinated modules: (i)~\textbf{ST-GCAT}, a schedule-conditioned spatio-temporal graph attention Transformer that predicts AP-level load up to 30 minutes ahead by cross-attending multi-step transit timetables with spatial AP interference topologies; (ii)~multi-agent \textbf{Soft Actor-Critic (SAC)} agents per AP that perform proactive, multi-objective control over transmit power, channel selection, and quantized 802.11e EDCA airtime quotas under a Lagrangian airtime constraint; and (iii)~\textbf{HierFed-KD}, a hierarchical federated distillation protocol that combines intra-zone parameter averaging with inter-zone proximal knowledge distillation over a public reference set, which keeps per-user data and replay buffers at the AP (data minimisation, not a formal privacy guarantee) and reduces inter-zone communication.

To increase physical and protocol realism, our evaluation incorporates IEEE 802.11 MAC contention based on Bianchi's saturation model, channel-switching outage penalties, and EDCA-based airtime budgeting. Over three training seeds, the proposed configuration of STAG-FedSAC (which feeds compact schedule features, not the latent forecaster embedding, to the policy) matches the static incumbent plan SSF in reward ($\mPropReward$ against $\mSSFReward$, a difference within seed variation) and reduces the average user latency to $\mPropLat$~ms ($11.8\%$ below SSF at $\mSSFLat$~ms); its throughput ($\mPropTput$~Mbps/user), QoS satisfaction ($\mPropQos$) and energy efficiency ($\mPropEeff$~Mbps/W) are comparable to those of SSF. Relative to the learned baselines it improves the reward by $56.9\%$ (FedDDPG) and $68.5\%$ (LSTM-DRL) and reduces latency by $40.5\%$ and $44.7\%$; these baselines change the channel of $0.67$--$0.75$ of the APs per interval, against $0.15$ for STAG-FedSAC and $0.075$ for SSF. ST-GCAT reduces the load-forecast RMSE relative to persistence by $35\%$ overall and $39\%$ in surge windows, and relative to the same predictor without the schedule by $31\%$ and $33\%$ (at the one-step horizon, simple forecasters are more accurate). HierFed-KD reduces inter-zone wide-area communication by $\commRatioAFive\times$ relative to weight-based hierarchical federation and $\commRatioFlat\times$ relative to flat FedAvg. In our ablations, feeding the forecaster's latent embedding to the policy and the joint predictor--policy gradient did not improve control. All results are from simulation.
\end{abstract}
