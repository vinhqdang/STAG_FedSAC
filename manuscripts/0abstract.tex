\begin{abstract}
Transit venues have predictable congestion: timetables announce when and where crowds will gather. We study whether this information can improve WiFi resource management and propose STAG-FedSAC, which combines a schedule-conditioned spatio-temporal graph attention forecaster (ST-GCAT), per-AP Soft Actor-Critic agents that choose power, channel and per-class airtime, and a two-level federation (zone averaging plus inter-zone distillation of policy outputs on a public reference set). We evaluate it in a simulator that, in contrast to the first version of this paper, models MAC contention (Bianchi), a channel-switching cost and a quantised airtime mapping onto 802.11 access categories; ignoring contention understates latency by about a factor of 1.8. All results are generated from logged runs. The timetable-conditioned forecaster reduces the load-forecast RMSE by \redFullPersistRmse\% relative to persistence and by \redFullATwoRmse\% relative to the same predictor without the schedule (surge windows: \redFullATwoSurgeRmse\%); at the one-step horizon, simple forecasters are more accurate. The learned federated agents, which start from the operator's configuration, are stable and clearly better than learned DDPG baselines (reward \mFullReward{} against \mFedDDPGReward{} and \mLSTMDRLReward), but they do not outperform the static configuration (\mSSFReward) in this simulator, and neither feeding the forecaster's embedding to the policy nor the joint predictor--policy gradient showed a benefit. Federation improves on isolated agents, and distillation-based inter-zone exchange needs \commRatioAFive$\times$ less wide-area traffic than weight-based hierarchical federation at a similar reward. Results are simulation-only, based on three training seeds, and the limitations are discussed in detail.
\end{abstract}
