\begin{abstract}
High-density transit venues---such as airport terminals, railway stations, and bus interchanges---experience severe, schedule-driven congestion where passenger crowds gather predictably around arrival and departure events. Traditional WLAN controllers operate reactively, balancing radio resources only after congestion causes packet loss and SLA violations. In this work, we propose \textbf{STAG-FedSAC}, an end-to-end framework for proactive, schedule-driven WiFi radio resource management. STAG-FedSAC integrates three coordinated modules: (i)~\textbf{ST-GCAT}, a schedule-conditioned spatio-temporal graph attention Transformer that predicts AP-level load up to 30 minutes ahead by cross-attending multi-step transit timetables with spatial AP interference topologies; (ii)~multi-agent \textbf{Soft Actor-Critic (SAC)} agents per AP that perform proactive, multi-objective control over transmit power, channel selection, and quantized 802.11e EDCA airtime quotas under a Lagrangian airtime constraint; and (iii)~\textbf{HierFed-KD}, a hierarchical federated distillation protocol that combines intra-zone parameter averaging with inter-zone proximal knowledge distillation over a public reference set, preserving multi-operator data privacy while drastically cutting communication overhead.

To ensure physical and protocol realism, our evaluation incorporates IEEE 802.11 MAC contention based on Bianchi's saturation model, channel-switching outage penalties, and hardware-aligned EDCA airtime budgeting. Across multi-seed evaluations, STAG-FedSAC achieves superior performance across all operational metrics: it attains a mean user throughput of $\mPropTput$~Mbps, reduces average user latency to $\mPropLat$~ms (an $11.8\%$ reduction compared to the static incumbent plan SSF at $\mSSFLat$~ms), and boosts QoS satisfaction to $\mPropQos$, with excellent energy efficiency ($\mPropEeff$~Mbps/W) and stable channel management ($0.15$ switches/AP/step). Relative to learned baselines, STAG-FedSAC outperforms FedDDPG and LSTM-DRL by $+56.9\%$ and $+68.5\%$ in reward and reduces latency by $40.5\%$ and $44.7\%$, respectively, avoiding the severe channel-switching thrashing that undermines non-entropy-regularized policies. Furthermore, ST-GCAT cuts load forecast RMSE by $32\%$ overall and $39.5\%$ in critical surge windows relative to persistence, outperforming schedule-unaware models by over $23\%$. HierFed-KD reduces inter-zone wide-area communication by $\commRatioAFive\times$ relative to weight-based hierarchical federation and $\commRatioFlat\times$ relative to flat FedAvg. Extensive ablation and sensitivity analyses provide concrete design principles for schedule-aware wireless reinforcement learning.
\end{abstract}
