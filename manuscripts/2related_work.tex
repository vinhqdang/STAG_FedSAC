\section{Related Works}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Spatio-Temporal Prediction for Wireless and Wi-Fi Networks}
Accurate prediction of access-point (AP) load and wireless traffic is a prerequisite for proactive resource management, and spatio-temporal deep learning has become the dominant approach. Shaabanzadeh and S\'anchez-Gonz\'alez~\cite{shaabanzadeh2024} evaluate a broad family of architectures (CNN, GRU, LSTM and Transformer) for spatio-temporal Wi-Fi load prediction at the individual AP level on real campus measurements spanning 100 APs over three months, fusing spatial neighbour information only when inter-AP correlations are statistically significant.

At cellular scale, Cai et al.~\cite{cai2024} propose DBSTGNN-Att, a dual-branch spatio-temporal graph neural network with self-attention that fuses location and point-of-interest features through an attributed $k$-nearest-neighbour graph, with superior short-, mid- and long-term predictions on the Milan Telecom Italia dataset. Lin et al.~\cite{lin2024} unify normal, target-alarm and neighbour-alarm network states by transfer learning over spatio-temporal graph neural networks for multi-scenario cellular KPI prediction. Xiao et al.~\cite{xiao2025} combine a diffusion convolutional GRU with multi-head attention to capture spatial diffusion, temporal periodicity and external influence in cellular traffic.

The work closest in spirit to ours is STECA-GCN~\cite{liu2024stgnn}, which incorporates an event dimension via cross-attention in a spatio-temporal graph model and feeds the output into a DDPG-based load-balancing agent; its event features are, however, coarse binary workday/holiday labels. Among the works we reviewed, we did not find one that uses structured transit timetable data (arrival and departure times and passenger counts) as cross-attention context for Wi-Fi AP load prediction; this is the setting of our ST-GCAT predictor.

\subsection{Deep Reinforcement Learning for Wireless Resource Allocation}
Deep reinforcement learning has been widely applied to continuous resource-management problems in wireless networks. Yang et al.~\cite{yang2024aro} identify a discounted-reward mismatch in prior wireless RL formulations and propose ARO-SAC, an average-reward Soft Actor-Critic variant for RAN slicing, with a 15\% gain over conventional SAC on the NetworkGym simulator. For energy efficiency in RIS-assisted UAV systems, Adhikari et al.~\cite{adhikari2024} jointly optimise phase shifts and 3D trajectory with twin delayed DDPG, benchmarking against SAC, DDPG and double DQN. Wang et al.~\cite{wang2024} address 6G mmWave massive MIMO-NOMA with a hybrid DRL framework that pairs dueling DQN for discrete subchannel allocation with DDPG for continuous power allocation. Pi and Ren~\cite{pi2024} develop HMSAC, a heterogeneous-action multi-agent SAC with an embedded attention mechanism that jointly optimises channels, power levels and bandwidths for NOMA uplink in vehicular networks. Qi et al.~\cite{qi2024} formulate a multi-QoS DRL problem for RIS-aided IoV networks that minimises age of information under vehicle-to-vehicle reliability constraints. In the works we reviewed, the environment state is a scalar or flat vector (channel gains, per-slice metrics, positions); we did not find a work that builds the RL state from the dense, spatially structured embedding of a spatio-temporal predictor, which is what our Graph-SAC state does.

\subsection{Federated Learning for Wireless Networks}
Azimi-Abarghouyi and Fodor~\cite{azimi2024} introduce scalable hierarchical over-the-air federated learning with a two-level cluster/global aggregation scheme and convergence bounds for heterogeneous wireless topologies. Pervej et al.~\cite{pervej2024} address bandwidth scarcity and device heterogeneity in hierarchical FL by adaptive model pruning during inter-level aggregation. At the aggregation-mechanism level, Shao et al.~\cite{shao2024} propose Selective-FD, which selectively shares soft-label knowledge without a well-trained teacher, and Pang et al.~\cite{pang2024} discuss how federated learning and knowledge distillation can be combined in wireless systems. Liang et al.~\cite{liang2025} jointly optimise communication energy and training latency in over-the-air FL with a DRL algorithm, and Wu et al.~\cite{wu2024} study age-based client selection and resource allocation for FL over NOMA networks. Li et al.~\cite{li2025kd} use knowledge distillation for asynchronous federated reinforcement learning in IoT. The hierarchical schemes above average or prune parameters, Selective-FD is formulated for classification, and the FL-wireless co-design works use flat FedAvg-style aggregation. We did not find a scheme that combines zone-level averaging, inter-zone distillation of \emph{policy outputs} on a public reference set and Wi-Fi resource optimisation as the target application; we do not claim that none exists.

\subsection{Graph Neural Networks in Networking}
Dai et al.~\cite{dai2024} survey graph-based resource management in wireless networks (GCN, GAT, GraphSAGE, MPNN) and identify GNN-enabled DRL as a major open direction. Liu et al.~\cite{liu2024gnn} integrate graph attention with multi-agent RL to address partial observability and non-stationarity. Ji et al.~\cite{ji2024} combine GraphSAGE with DRL so that low-dimensional graph embeddings serve directly as the distributed agents' state for channel selection and power control in C-V2X. Marwani and Kaddoum~\cite{marwani2024} model multi-link interference as a graph and train a GNN to solve power control and spectrum allocation jointly, and Li et al.~\cite{li2024hetgnn} use a heterogeneous GNN with distinct node and edge types for cell-free massive MIMO power allocation.

Ji et al.~\cite{ji2024} is architecturally the closest to ours, since GNN embeddings are the RL state; it uses a static interference-topology convolution rather than a forecast-conditioned spatio-temporal embedding, employs DQN rather than SAC, and has no federated training. We did not find prior work that passes the output of a schedule-conditioned graph forecaster into the state of a SAC policy.
