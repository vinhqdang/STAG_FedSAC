All variants use the same agents, reward, protocol and seeds and differ only in how base layers are shared; Table~\ref{tab:fed} lists their held-out performance and the measured traffic of the whole training run.

\paragraph{Effect of federation} Yes, moderately. Isolated agents obtain a reward of $\mIsoReward$ (latency $\mIsoLat$~ms); zone-level averaging alone $\mAFourReward$, flat FedAvg $\mFlatReward$, weight-based hierarchical federation $\mAFiveReward$ and the proposed distillation-based federation $\mFullReward$. All federated variants are above the isolated agents, by about $0.01$--$0.03$, which supports sharing what the agents learn. The differences \emph{among} the federated variants are of the order of the seed-to-seed variation (two to three seeds), so we cannot rank them and make no claim that hierarchical distillation is more accurate than flat averaging or than weight-based hierarchical federation.

\paragraph{Pooled experience is not an upper bound} The comparator in which each agent samples its minibatches from the experience of \emph{all} APs, which violates the privacy motivation, obtains only $\mCentralReward$, below isolated learning. A plausible reason is that the policies are AP-specific (different channels, loads and neighbourhoods), so transitions of other APs are off-distribution for an agent's own critic. We therefore cannot use it to quantify a ``price of privacy'' in this setting.

\paragraph{Communication} Over a whole run, flat FedAvg between all APs and a central server moves $\commWanFlat$~MB over the wide-area link. Weight-based hierarchical federation reduces this to $\commWanAFive$~MB (only the zone models travel between zones and venue controller); the distillation-based Level~2 of HierFed-KD to $\commWanFull$~MB, of which $\commRefMB$~MB are the reference states shipped to the zones and $\commOutMB$~MB the soft outputs, i.e.\ $\commRatioAFive\times$ less than weight-based hierarchical federation and $\commRatioFlat\times$ less than flat FedAvg, at a similar reward. Traffic inside zones is unchanged ($\commIntraFull$~MB per run) because Level~1 exchanges weights in every hierarchical variant. These ratios replace the single ``$11.5\times$'' figure of the first version, which was not measured; they depend on the choices made here ($M=100$ reference states, refresh every five global rounds, $T_{\mathrm{global}}=500$) and count only payload bytes.
