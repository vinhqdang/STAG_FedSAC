All federation variants share identical per-AP SAC agents, reward formulations, simulation environments, and random seeds, differing solely in how model parameters and knowledge are shared across the network hierarchy. Table~\ref{tab:fed} and Figure~\ref{fig:fed} summarize their held-out evaluation performance alongside exact byte-level communication measurements collected across the entire training campaign.

\paragraph{Benefits of Federated Collaboration} 
Sharing learned representations across the network hierarchy provides clear performance benefits over isolated learning. Isolated agents operating without federation achieve a reward of $\mIsoReward$ and an average latency of $\mIsoLat$~ms. In contrast, all federated variants achieve higher rewards ($0.132$--$0.146$) and reduced latency ($156.9$--$176.9$~ms). By sharing base feature representations, federated APs benefit from the collective experience of neighbouring APs experiencing diverse congestion conditions, establishing the tangible value of multi-AP collaborative learning in transit venues.

\paragraph{Failure of Monolithic Experience Pooling} 
To examine whether unconstrained centralized data pooling could serve as an empirical upper bound, we evaluate a centralized learner that samples transitions indiscriminately from a single pooled replay buffer across all APs. Crucially, this pooled-experience approach achieves a reward of only $\mCentralReward$ and an elevated latency of $\mCentralLat$~ms, performing significantly worse than even isolated learning. Because individual APs occupy heterogeneous spatial positions with distinct channel assignments, local contention domains, and traffic mixes, naive experience pooling creates severe policy-distribution mismatch for each agent's critic. This demonstrates that multi-AP coordination requires decentralized, personalized agent policies rather than monolithic data pooling.

\paragraph{Bandwidth Efficiency and Privacy Boundaries} 
While achieving competitive control performance, HierFed-KD provides dramatic communication reductions across the wide-area inter-zone network. Flat FedAvg requires exchanging full model weights directly with a central controller, consuming $\commWanFlat$~MB of wide-area traffic over training. Weight-based hierarchical federation reduces this to $\commWanAFive$~MB by aggregating models at zone controllers before global synchronization.

HierFed-KD slashes wide-area inter-zone communication to only $\commWanFull$~MB total ($\commRefMB$~MB for periodic public reference states and $\commOutMB$~MB for soft policy distributions). This constitutes an exact $\commRatioAFive\times$ bandwidth reduction relative to weight-based hierarchical federation and a $\commRatioFlat\times$ reduction relative to flat FedAvg. Furthermore, because only soft policy distributions on an unlabelled public reference set are transmitted across administrative zones, HierFed-KD maintains rigorous data minimization, preventing the exposure of local replay transitions, per-user identifiers, and raw model weights across multi-operator boundaries.
